# English figure and table captions

## Table 1. Baseline characteristics of the study population and participants with assistance gaps

Counts are unweighted; percentages are survey weighted within each column and may not total 100 because of rounding. Continuous summaries are survey weighted. The 564 participants with gaps are a subset of the overall population; no between-column tests were performed. A gap requires an activity reported as needing help or impossible to perform and explicit non-receipt of help for that activity. Family assistance denotes a confirmed helper report and may coexist with a gap; other records do not establish absence of family help. ADL, activities of daily living; IADL, instrumental ADL; IQR, interquartile range; SD, standard deviation. Measurement and source-availability details are in Table S1.

## Table 2. Allocation and resource consequences at 500 complete programmes per 1,000 candidates

S0, function-first; S1, gap-first. Both allocate 500 complete programmes per 1,000 original, survey-weighted candidates; S1 additionally pays for targeted enquiry. Differences are S1 minus S0, with paired percentile 95% CIs from community resampling; structural equalities are shown as 0. Nursing and patient time include enquiry. Time and 2024 US$ costs are planning estimates. Family assistance means confirmed reports (Table S1). Allocations are modelled opportunities, not observed counts; covered need is not functional improvement.

## Table S3. Functional and family profiles of gainers and displaced candidates at equal 500 places

The 53 gaining and 49 losing source records contribute changes in allocation probability, not simple participant averages. Each side represents 17.40 weighted opportunities per 1,000 original candidates. Profiles and CIs are weighted by allocation-probability changes. All released intervals use 5,000 valid paired community draws except recorded family-help hours for gaining records (4,974). Withheld cells and protected valid-draw counts remain undisclosed. Family-help hours are recorded assistance, not estimated time savings.

## Table S4. Paired allocation differences across nine capacity scenarios

S1 minus S0, per 1,000 original candidates; 5,000 paired community draws. Equal places assumes information already known; fixed resources charges targeted enquiry to the corresponding capacity ceilings. Structural zeros are shown as 0. Protected cells are withheld, not zero. The IADL difference is an index total, not patient deterioration. Figure 2 reverses its sign to show the need-coverage loss.

## Table S5. Information acquisition, rural constraints and expansion requirements

Per 1,000 original candidates; costs are 2024 US$. Enquiry rows compare S1 targeted (alternative) with universal (reference) enquiry; rural fixed-resource rows compare S1 with S0. No-displacement expansion retains original S0 opportunities and adds complete programmes. Rural restoration compares S1 resources for 500 programmes with the original ceilings, NOT rural S1 with S0. Totals add stored increments to unrounded constants; intervals are the original paired increments (or the stored package-total interval translated by 500). Rural travel adds 0.5 h to each of four nursing and six OT visits. OT binds the next-programme step in all 5,000 community and county draws; RN also binds in two county draws for S1. Money alone cannot replace staff time.

## Table S6. Reallocation after reassessment of needs in 2020

Per 1,000 original 2018 candidates. The same 1,386 observable eligible participants represent 557.16 original opportunities; half is 278.58, not 500. Reference and alternative are S0 and S1 except the explicitly labelled updating rows, which compare frozen 2018 with updated 2020 functional priority. No failures or capacity shortfalls occurred in the original 5,000 community or county resamples of these contrasts. Unknown qualification is not absence of need. Current-eligibility compatibility bounds are 557.16–632.45; they are not CIs. This is natural reassessment, not a programme treatment effect.

## Table S7. Age, enquiry duration and clustering sensitivities

## Figure 1. Service coverage across care-capacity scenarios

S0, function-first; S1, gap-first. Both panels compare S0 and S1 with equal complete programme places and assistance information already known. A shows the proportion of all candidates with an explicit same-activity assistance gap allocated a programme, not the proportion of all candidates. The 500-place reference covers 76.5% and 84.0% of the gap group, respectively. B shows the paired difference in gap allocations per 1,000 original candidates. Nodes are the nine calculated capacities; straight segments only guide the eye between adjacent disclosed nodes. The shaded guide joins pointwise paired percentile 95% CIs from 5,000 community resamples; it is not a simultaneous band or an interval for uncalculated capacities. S1 values and differences at 100, 200 and 700 remain protected. Zeros at 800 and 900 are released results. See Table S4.

## Figure 2. Trade-offs between assistance-gap coverage and functional need

Points show S1 minus S0 across released capacity scenarios, comparing equal programme places with information known (A) and the same nursing, occupational therapy and budget ceilings with targeted enquiry charged (B). The horizontal axis is additional gap allocations; the vertical axis reverses the sign of the covered-IADL difference, so positive values indicate less baseline IADL need covered. Both use 1,000 original candidates. Capacity labels identify each point; 800 and 900 coincide at the origin without artificial displacement. Only the 500-place reference is shaded: each 2-opportunity by 10-index-unit cell counts actual paired differences in the existing 5,000 community draws. No new resampling, smoothing or fitted ellipse is used. The shading is not a calibrated joint 95% confidence region; marginal paired CIs are in Table S4. Different capacities have different resource envelopes, so geometric position does not establish dominance. Neither axis is a patient health outcome. See Figure S2 for the existing conditional sampling-stability grid.

## Figure 3. Support-enquiry workload and modelled programme shortfalls

A compares universal and targeted five-minute support enquiries within S1 at equal 500 complete programmes. Targeted enquiry uses 75.94 fewer nursing hours; ranking-information work is distinct from necessary clinical assessment. B shows shortfalls from 500 under the original ceilings of 4,000 nursing hours, 5,750 occupational therapy hours and 2024 US$539,277.92 per 1,000 candidates. Bar-end labels show reductions of 0, 1, 11, 63 and 64; the corresponding feasible totals from top to bottom are 500, 499, 489, 437 and 436 programmes, not observed service delivery. Hatched bars add 30 minutes of rural travel per visit, separately for four nursing and six OT visits. Rural S1 uses targeted enquiry. Error bars are pointwise 95% CIs from the frozen community resamples. The separate next-programme diagnostic identifies OT as the marginal rural bottleneck; this is not inferred from bar heights. Enquiry and rural travel are distinct resource inputs. All quantities retain the original 1,000-candidate denominator; patient participation, actual expenditure and expansion requirements are reported in Table S5.

## Figure S1. Study population and observed follow-up qualification

All counts are unweighted records from the frozen sample flow. Weighted 2020 quantities retain the original 2018 denominator of 1,000 candidates. The five current-qualification categories are mutually exclusive and sum to 2,433 baseline participants. Unresolved qualification is neither independence nor absence of need. A separate unknown-life-or-function category overlaps qualification categories and is not added to this partition. No future survival or recovery was used to select the baseline population.

## Figure S2. Conditional sampling stability of allocation requirements at the 500-place reference

Colours give the fraction of the existing 5,000 paired community draws meeting both the indicated minimum additional gap allocations and maximum loss of covered IADL need. A retains equal programme places with information known; B retains the original professional and budget ceilings, charging targeted enquiry. The entire displayed threshold grid was already calculated in the frozen analysis; no new value weights, thresholds or policy optimisation are introduced. Cells are shown without interpolation. These fractions describe the sampling stability of two specified allocation requirements, not posterior probabilities, cost-effectiveness acceptability, clinical effectiveness or net social benefit. Family and other resource consequences require separate consideration.
